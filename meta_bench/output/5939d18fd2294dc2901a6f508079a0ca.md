# Abstract  

Indian cattle breeds (Bos indicus) are renowned for their resilience in diverse environments and resistance to infections compared to Bos taurus. Although recent studies have explored the genetic basis of disease resistance in cattle, comprehensive analyses aimed at identifying the genomic signatures associated with disease-resistance traits in Indian cattle are still unclear. A genome-wide comparison between Bos indicus and Bos taurus could uncover key genomic elements related to disease-resistance traits or immunity. This study aimed to conduct chromosome-by-chromosome analyses among Bos genomes using three pairwise combinations: Bos taurus (Hereford) vs. Bos indicus (Nelore breed), Bos taurus (Hereford) vs. Bos indicus (Gir), and Bos indicus (Nelore) vs. Bos indicus (Gir) to investigate genomic variations focusing in immunity genes and quantitative trait loci (QTLs). Comparative genomic analysis was performed using SyMAP, GSAAlign, and SyRI. An in-house Python script was utilized for gene annotation and visualization. Statistical differences in genomic variations among breed pairs were assessed using ANOVA. Gene Ontology (GO) and pathway enrichment analysis were carried out using DAVID for each variant type. The analysis revealed non-syntenic regions associated with four immunity genes in Nelore: VSTM1L, Galectin-9, HDAC5, and POLH. Significant genomic differences were observed on chromosomes 12, 24, 28, 29 and X. Chromosome X of the Nelore lost 50 Mbps of genomic regions compared to Hereford, although all genes remained intact. Further exploration of translocation events on Chromosome X in the Nelore revealed that chromosomes 28 had more single nucleotide variants (SNVs), insertions, and deletions. In total, 8, 16, and innate immunity genes in Nelore were observed with longer insertions, deletions, and substitutions respectively with 62 genes were had all three variations with statistical significance. QTL analysis indicated these variations were primarily linked to milk and health traits. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses revealed that genes with substitution variants were significantly enriched in immune-related processes, including NF-kappa B signaling, cytokine production, and T cell receptor signaling. Several key immune genes, such as TLR4, TLR9, RIPK2, PLCG2, FADD, CLEC7A, RELA, CD40, TRIM25, IRAK1/2/3, NOD2, BCL2, and CARD9, were involved in multiple biological process, highlighting the impact of structural variations on immune function in Nelore cattle. Overall, this study highlights genomic variations in innate immunity genes in Nelore, its associated QTLs, biological process and pathways. This finding lays the groundwork for future research to identify the immune markers related to disease-resistance traits in indicine cattle.

# Introduction  

Indicine cattle (members of Bos indicus), such as zebu cattle, are native to the Indian subcontinent. Accounting for about 80% of India's total cattle population, they offer various economic advantages over taurine cattle (members of B. taurus), particularly when raised under tropical conditions [1, 2]. The native breeds are crucial to the country's dairy industry, which has made India a global leader in milk production, and supports over 80 million rural households. Currently, 50 distinct breeds of indicine cattle are registered in India, evolved through both natural selection and selective breeding for adaptation to different climatic conditions are registered in India. These breeds can cope with extreme heat or similar adverse climatic conditions, survive despite poor nutrition, and are resistant to several diseases [3, 4, 5, 6]. One notable example is 'Vechur' (named after a village of the same name), the smallest cattle breed in the world, found in the Kottayam district of Kerala. It is well-known for its greater resistance to viral, bacterial, and parasitic diseases than that shown by other exotic breeds and crossbred cattle [7].  

The innate immune response, which kicks in at the earliest stages of infection in mammals, protects them from pathogenic infections. This response is non-specific to the pathogen [8, 9, 10]. Therefore, understanding the differences between the immune systems of indicine and taurine cattle could provide valuable insights into disease resistance shown by members of Bos indicus. This disease resistance trait is essential for better management of herds and breeding development strategies [11, 12]. In particular, exploring the genomic regions responsible for disease resistance and susceptibility variations opens new opportunities to identify significant genomic signatures for future molecular breeding studies [13].  

Recent research on livestock improvement is increasingly focused on whole-genome data to detect genetic factors or genomic signatures associated with important traits such as disease resistance. This includes identifying such structural variations such as insertions, deletions, duplications, inversions, translocations, and other structural variations present on genomic regions by comparing indicine and taurine breeds. Two relevant sources of assembled whole-genome data at chromosome level of cattle are (1) the first whole-genome sequence of Bos indicus cattle (Nelore breed) with 52X coverage by the SOLiD sequencing platform with a short-read length of 25–50 bases [14] and (2) the whole-genome sequencing data of Bos taurus cattle with multiple improvements, such as covering 91 % of the genome, closing gaps, correcting errors, removing bacterial contigs, and identifying the first portion of Y chromosomes using whole-genome shotgun sequencing methods [15]. Recently, a comparative whole-genome analysis of several Bos indicus breeds (Kangayam, Tharparkar, Sahiwal, Red Sindhi, and Hariana) and of Bos taurus cattle identified a greater number of genetic variations. This study revealed over 155 million single-nucleotide polymorphisms  

(SNPs) and more than one million InDels among a total of 17,252 genes [16]. Among these genes, many involved in innate immune responses were associated with key pathways such as toll-like receptor signalling, retinoic acid-inducible gene I-like receptor signalling, NOD-like receptor signalling, and the Jak-STAT pathway. The same study identified many missense variants in genes such as TLR3 and TLR4, transcription factors such as IRFs (IRF3/IRF7) and NF-kB, and non-synonymous variants in genes such as MyD88, IRAK4, RIG-I, TRIM25, MAVS, NOD1, and NOD2 [16]. Furthermore, draft de novo genome assemblies and mitochondrial genome assemblies of Bos indicus breeds such as Ongole, Kasargod Dwarf, Kasargod Kapila, and Vechur were generated using Illumina short-read technology. An analysis of the exon-intron structure of 15 key genes related to bovine traits including milk quality, metabolism, and immune response revealed structural variation in their exon-intron numbers. Notably, seven of these genes had fewer exons in Ongole than in Bos taurus. Among those genes, PFKP and GPX4 are involved in glycolysis regulation and immune response [17].  

Despite multiple studies exploring the genetic basis of disease resistance in cattle genomics [18, 19, 20, 21, 22], a genome-wide comparison between assembled chromosome-level genomes of Bos indicus and Bos taurus remains limited and requires additional investigation. To bridge that gap, we conducted a detailed chromosome-by-chromosome analysis of the Bos genomes available with the National Centre for Biotechnology (NCBI), focusing specifically on Bos indicus cattle – zebu cattle such as Nelore (Bos_indicus_1.0, RefSeq: GCF_000247795.1) and Gir (ASM293397v1) – and Bos taurus cattle, namely Hereford (ARS-UCD2.0, RefSeq: GCF_002263795.3). We compared members of three distinct pairs, namely Bos taurus (Hereford) and Bos indicus (Nelore), Bos taurus (Hereford) and Bos indicus (Gir), and Bos indicus (Nelore) and Bos indicus (Gir), to obtain fresh insights into the genetic basis of the disease resistance in Bos indicus cattle.  

# Materials and methods  

# General workflow  

The flowchart (Fig. 1) outlines the steps in this comparative study of the Bos genome. Initially, whole-genome sequence data were collected for three cattle breeds, namely Nelore, Gir (both from Bos indicus), and Hereford (Bos taurus). The data were compared for the three pairs specified earlier and also shown in Fig. 1, and genomic variations were identified using Synteny Mapping and Analysis Program (SyMAP) [23], GSAalign [24], and Synteny and Rearrangement Identifier (SyRI) [25]. Because SyMAP provides data on syntenic regions and alignment statistics but without detailed data on variation, further analysis was performed using GSAalign and SyRI. Common chromosomal variations predicted by both GSAalign and SyRI were identified, and Nelore protein-coding data were used to identify genes and immunity genes associated with these variations. Quantitative trait loci (QTLs) were mapped to the genes, and immunity genes were identified within the common chromosomal variations. Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was performed for the genes carrying common chromosomal variations using DAVID [26, 27].  

# Sample preparation  

The whole-genome assembly data were downloaded from the NCBI in both GenBank and FASTA formats for the three breeds, namely Nelore (Accession ID: GCA_000247795.2), Gir (Accession ID: GCA_002933975.1), and Hereford (Accession ID: GCA_002263795.3). The  

assembly statistics were analyzed for the number of chromosomes assembled, scaffold N50 values, GC percentage, and genome coverage using QUAST [28]. The N50 values indicating length of the contig where all contigs of at least together to cover at least 50 % of the assembly in comparison to the reference genome, along with length greater than 100Mb was considered for the analysis. The data for all chromosomes from the assemblies of the three breeds were subsequently extracted for further analysis.  

# Comparative genomic analysis  

To perform comparative genomic analysis among the selected breeds of cattle, we conducted pairwise comparison using one from Bos taurus, namely Hereford, and two from Bos indicus, namely Nelore and Gir. The three pairwise comparison were Hereford–Nelore, Hereford–Gir, and Nelore–Gir. In each pairwise analysis, the genome of the former breed was designated as the reference, and the latter one as the query. The comparisons focused on variation in terms of the length, number, and function of genetic variants. In addition, interchromosomal variations were identified as revealed by comparing chromosomes within the same breed, particularly the X chromosome, and autosomes, to detect the translocations of genomic elements from the X chromosome to other autosomes within the Bos genomes. For the chromosome-by-chromosome analysis, we used three comparative genomics tools, namely SyMAP, GSAIg, and SyRI. These tools were employed primarily to analyze the query genome against the reference genome of bovines' chromosome by chromosome.  

# Analysis using SyMAP  

SyMAP was used for detecting and analyzing syntenic and non-syntenic regions between the query and the reference chromosomes of the selected genomes [23]. SyMAP integrates MUMmer, a high-performance alignment tool using Perl, with the core SyMAP program running on Java. SyMAP requires genome sequences in FASTA format and corresponding annotation files in GFF3 format for both query and reference genomes. Because an annotation file for Gir genome was unavailable, we used Bos indicus (Nelore) as the query genome and Bos taurus (Hereford) as the reference genome. The chromosome-level FASTA files and the corresponding annotation files for both the query and the reference genomes were uploaded and SyMAP was run with default settings. SyMAP, in turn, generated synteny maps, dot plots of aligned chromosomes, and basic alignment statistics including coverage, annotations, and gene content. However, SyMAP does not produce a VCF (variant call format) file or any detailed variant information but merely provides a CSV (comma-separated values) file containing data on the percentage of hits, identity, similarity, and gene products from both the query and the reference genomes. Therefore, to identify genes with lower sequence similarity, we used a Python script created in house to extract genes showing less than 60% sequence similarity between the indicine and the taurine groups from the alignment output files.  

# Analysis using GSAIg  

GSAIgns efficiently aligns larger query and reference genomes by indexing the reference genome using the Burrows-Wheeler Transform (BWT) in Bowtie2. GSAIgns employs a parallelized, divide-and-conquer approach to rapidly construct alignments and identify gapped regions. We used GSAIgns to compare the members of each of the three pairs of breeds as mentioned above and also to compare the chromosomes of the same breed to investigate translocations of genetic elements. Specifically, we compared chromosomes X and Y with autosomes in the Bos genome to identify any translocation events. The input for GSAIgns consisted of whole-genome data on the query and the reference genomes in FASTA format, with the reference file indexed in Bowtie2 [24]. We executed the GSAIgns script, specifying the appropriate query and reference genomes in FASTA format. GSAIgns performed the alignment and generated output files in mutation annotation format (MAF) or clustal format (ALN) and VCF files containing the identified sequence variants. An in-house Python script was used to parse these output files to quantify variations such as single nucleotide variants (SNVs), insertions, and deletions.  

# Analysis using SyRI  

SyRI was used for detecting genomic differences between related genomes using whole-genome assemblies. SyRI identifies syntenic regions, structural rearrangements, and local variations [25]. As before, we analyzed the three pairs of chromosomes by chromosome. In each comparison, the former genome served as the reference and the latter, as the query. We ran SyRI by specifying the appropriate query and the reference genomes in FASTA format within the script file. SyRI generated the alignment file in sequence alignment map (SAM) format using its integrated minimap2 tool and produced output files in tab-separated values (TSV) and VCF formats. The output included annotations on 20 different parameters and a visual representation of genomic variations generated using the integrated plotsr tool [29]. The output files were further processed using a Python script generated in-house to count the occurrences of each variant across a given genome.  

# Validation  

The data generated by all three tools – SyMAP, GSAIg, and SyRI – were validated by cross-comparing the results from each tool and arriving at consensus results through the in-house Python script. Such validation was crucial to predicting and verifying genes and their functions within variable genomic regions. In comparing the genomes, SyMAP provides sequence-similarity information, GSAIg identifies three types of variations, and SyRI detects 20 distinct types of variations. To ensure high confidence variant detection, regions with poor assembly quality such as those containing excessive gaps or low alignment scores were excluded from the analysis. Furthermore, only chromosomal variations consistently identified by both GSAIg and SyRI were retained for downstream annotation, as SyMAP  

does not provide variant-level information. This approach minimized technical artifacts and enhanced the biological and functional relevance of the results. Identical variants identified by two different methods are considered more reliable. Therefore, we focused on insertions, deletions, and substitutions identified by both GSAI$^{\text{a}}$n and SyRI for variant annotation. Variant functional annotation was performed particularly in comparing Bos taurus and Bos indicus (Nelore), because annotations were unavailable for Gir.  

# Statistical Analysis  

To evaluate the statistical significance of genomic variation across the three pairwise breed comparisons (Hereford vs. Nelore, Hereford vs. Gir, and Nelore vs. Gir), one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed. The test was applied separately for each type of variants: insertions, deletions, and substitutions identified using GSAIg and SyRI. ANOVA was used to determine whether the normalized number of variants per chromosome significantly different. For example, for normalized insertion counts in chromosome 1 in one pairwise comparison was checked for the same chromosome in other pairwise comparisons. A higher F-value indicates greater variance between groups relative to within-group variance. Statistical significance was assessed using corresponding P-values, with values less than 0.05 considered significant.  

# Genome annotation from common chromosomal variations  

To annotate common chromosomal variations, we first identified structural variants detected by both GSAIg and SyRI. These overlapping variants were then analyzed using in-house Python scripts to locate genes within the Bos indicus reference genome (NCBI RefSeq assembly: GCF_000247795.1, Nelore) that harbored shared insertions, deletions, and substitutions. Although chromosome-level assembly data are available for the Gir breed, gene annotations have not yet been deposited in NCBI at the time of analysis. As a result, for the purpose of gene-level annotation of chromosomal variations, we focused on the pairwise comparison between Bos taurus (Hereford) and Bos indicus (Nelore), for which both complete assemblies and gene annotations are available. We applied eight specific criteria (outlined in Fig. 2) to filter and extract protein-coding genes associated with these variations in the Nelore genome. This approach resulted an annotated output containing detailed information on the genes associated with the variations.  

# Extracting immunity genes  

To identify the immune-related genes with structural variations, we employed a combined approach using both curated database and keyword-based search, implemented through an in-house Python script. First, we used the InnateDB database to identify innate immune genes with structural variations. This database contains a curated list of 1697 genes specifically involved in innate immunity. However, since InnateDB focuses solely on innate immunity, we aimed to capture other immune-related genes that may contribute to adaptive immunity or immune regulatory functions but are not included in the database. To achieve this, we conducted a keyword-based search on the annotated gene list derived from the  

genome data. The following immune-related keywords were used for the search: immunoglobulin, immunoreceptor, autoimmune, Toll-like receptor (TLR), IgG, autoimmune, autophagy, immunogen, immune, innate, T-cell, B-cell, lymphocyte, histocompatibility, CD24, CD4, LY96, IFIT3, PGLYRP1, NKG2D, UL16, leukocyte, cytokine, antimicrobial peptide, beta-defensin 2, IL15, IL2, and chemokine. This combined approach allowed for a more comprehensive identification of genes involved in immune-related functions.  

# Analysis of quantitative trait loci  

Using the genomic variations such as insertions, deletions, and substitutions, identified by both GSAIgN and SyRI between Nelore and Hereford, we proceeded to look at the QTLs associated with the genes that were found to have chromosomal variations including insertions, deletions, and substitutions, as identified by both GSAIgN and SyRI. We used the QTL data of the Hereford breed (ARS_UCD1.2) in GFF format obtained from the cattle QTLdb of the Animal QTLdb database (www.animalgenome.org). The QTL deposited in the database were related to characters associated with reproduction, production of milk, health, exterior, and meat and carcass. The locus of a qualitative trait is shown as a 4-bp-long segment, referred to as a QTL span. Using an in-house Python script, we identified the reported QTLs associated with various traits in the chromosomal regions showing structural variants.  

# Gene Ontology Enrichment Analysis:  

To investigate the functional relevance of genes with structural variants, we performed Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis separately for genes impacted by insertions, deletions, and substitutions. The analysis was conducted using the DAVID Bioinformatics Resources (v6.8). For each variant category, a list of variant genes was submitted, and GO terms were evaluated under the categories of Biological Process (BP), Molecular Function (MF), Cellular Component (CC), KEGG Pathways, and InterPro protein domains. Only significantly enriched terms with a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 were retained. We compared the significant GO terms across all three variant types to identify common functional signatures and selected those consistently enriched (p < 0.05) in each dataset. These significantly enriched terms were visualized using Python's matplotlib/seaborn.  

# Results  

# Reference Genome Statistics  

We used three reference genomes for comparative variant discovery: Bos taurus (ARS-UCD1.2, GCF_002263795.3, Hereford breed), Bos indicus (Bos_Indicus, Nelore breed), and Bos indicus (ASM293397v1, Gir breed). The Bos taurus reference consisted of 1,958 contigs, with a total length of 2.77 Gb, an N50 of 103.3 Mb, and minimal gap content (1.02 N's per 100 kbp). The Bos_Indicus assembly contained only 32 contigs and had the highest N50 (106.3 Mb), but a higher proportion of gaps (7,664.85 N's per 100 kbp). ASM293397v1 was more fragmented (23,400 contigs), with moderate gap content (1,657 N's per 100 kbp) and an N50 of 105.7 Mb. All three assemblies have high contiguity based on their N50 values. Despite the limitation in Bos indicus assembly, we incorporate them to particularly identify indicine-specific variants. To mitigate the impact of low-quality regions, we excluded poorly aligned or highly gapped regions and retained only the variants identified by both GSAAlign and SyRI tool for downstream annotation and analysis. This ensured the reliability and biological relevance of the identified variants.

# Statistical significance of variations  

To assess the statistical significance of genomic variation across the three breed comparisons (Hereford vs. Nelore, Hereford vs. Gir, and Nelore vs. Gir), we performed one-way ANOVA on the normalized number of insertions, deletions, and substitutions identified by GSAIg and SyRI. The GSAIg analysis revealed highly significant differences among the breed pairs for all three variant types: substitutions (F-value = 387.18, p-value = $4.88 \times 10^{-5}$), insertions (F-value = 494.03, p-value = $3.17 \times 10^{-5}$), and deletions (F-value = 1239.32, p-value = $1.17 \times 10^{-5}$). Similarly, SyRI analysis also indicated statistically significant differences for insertions (F = 458.38, p-value = $6.28 \times 10^{-5}$), deletions (F = 1313.4, p-value = $1.01 \times 10^{-5}$), and to a lesser extent for substitutions (F = 8.02, p-value = $6.32 \times 10^{-5}$).  

In ANOVA, a higher F-value reflects a greater difference in means between group relative to the variations within groups, suggesting stronger evidence of true differences. A smaller P-value (typically < 0.05) indicates strong statistical significance. All observed P-values in this analysis were extremely low, indicating highly significant differences in the number of variants among the breed comparisons. These findings confirm that structural variants, particularly insertions and deletions differ significantly among Nelore, Gir, and Hereford cattle, indicating considerable genomic divergence among the breeds.

# Discussion  

The current study sought to explore genomic variations between two Bos indicus breeds (Nelore and Gir) and one Bos taurus breed (Hereford), to identify unique features related to immunity and QTLs in indicine cattle. While the Bos taurus ARS-UCD1.2 reference genome is of high contiguity and reference-grade quality (1,958 contigs, N50 = 103.3 Mb, and only 1.02 N's per 100 kbp), we also incorporated two Bos indicus genomes such as Bos_indicus (32 contigs, N50 = 106.3 Mb, 7,664.85 N's/100 kbp) and ASM293397v1 (23,400 contigs, N50 = 105.7 Mb, 1,657 N's/100 kbp). Although these indicine assemblies exhibit limitations such as increased gap content or fragmentation, we included these genomes to identify indicine specific variant in the high confidence regions. We acknowledge that regions of lower assembly quality could influence variant detection in some loci. To address this, we applied good quality control measures such as excluded low-confidence and highly gapped regions, and focused on confidently aligned segments across all three genomes. In addition, we filter the high confidence variants which identified by two different tool such as GSAalign and SyRI. This approach helped ensure that the identified structural variants reflect true biological differences rather than assembly artifacts. The ANOVA result indicates the variants were statistically significant across three pairwise comparison.  

Our synteny analysis identified 13 genes in indicine cattle with less than 60% sequence identity compared to their taurine counterparts, based on pairwise alignment metrics generated by SyMAP [33, 34]. Among these, four genes were immune-related, suggesting potential divergence in immune function between breeds. One such gene, VSTM1L, is expressed in multiple immune cells, including T cells, B cells, natural killer cells, and dendritic cells. This gene plays a vital role in regulating immune cell activation, differentiation, and the expression of immunity-related proteins such as antibodies and cell surface receptors [35]. Another gene, Galectin-9, which is evolutionarily conserved, has been implicated in innate immune responses to bacterial infections and also plays a role in modulating cytotoxic immune responses by suppressing the activity of T cells and natural killer cells. [36]. Additionally, Histone deacetylase 5 (HDAC5) was identified as a gene involved in the regulation of cytokine signalling pathways and immune cell activity, as well as the development of immune-related diseases [37]. DNA polymerase eta (POLH), another gene identified in this study, is a specialized polymerase crucial to error-free bypass of DNA damage, is highly expressed in rapidly proliferating immune cells such as B and T lymphocytes, and plays a key role in the development and function of the immune system [38]. These findings highlight the genetic distinctions between indicine and taurine cattle, particularly in genes linked to immunity, suggesting the genetic make-up of the immune system in indicine cattle.  

Compared to taurine Hereford breed, Nelore cattle showed a significantly higher number of insertions on chromosomes 29, substitutions on chromosome 24 and deletions on chromosome X. Gir cattle exhibited a genomic differences on chromosome X with higher number of insertions and deletions, as well as substitutions on chromosome 24. These findings suggest chromosomes X exhibits notable genomic differences when compared to Hereford cattle. Although both Nelore and Gir are indicine breeds, the comparison between them revealed a higher number of insertions on chromosome 12, deletion on chromosome 29, and substitution on chromosome 28. Overall, substitutions were observed more frequently than insertions and deletions across all comparisons. A particularly noteworthy finding was the loss of approximately 50 Mbp of genomic regions on Chromosome X in Nelore. Despite 381 deletions across 52 genes and 1532 deletions in intergenic regions in Chromosome X, no deletions were observed in immunity-related genes. Chromosome X is the second largest in the genome and harbours many genes that can influence various phenotypes [39, 40], a feature attributed to higher linkage disequilibrium than that seen in autosomes [41]. To investigate whether the lost genomic regions from Chromosome X could have translocated to other autosomes, we compared Chromosome X with other chromosomes in Nelore. The comparison revealed that compared to Chromosome X, Chromosome 28 accounted for a higher number of substitutions, insertions and deletions. In addition, intrachromosomal insertions, deletions, and substitutions between Chromosome X and autosomes within each breed suggest a possibility of genome dose compensation among cattle breeds. A genome-wide association study (GWAS) aimed at identifying chromosome regions linked to sexual precocity in Nelore cattle found that the highest proportion of genetic variation for early pregnancy and scrotal circumference was concentrated on specific chromosomes. Large effects for early pregnancy were located on chromosomes 5, 6, 7, 14, 18, 21, and 27, whereas those for scrotal circumference were located on chromosomes 4, 8, 11, 13, 14, 19, 22, and 23 [40]. Notably, the region on Chromosome 13 associated with scrotal circumference had previously been linked to the phenotype of fat layer thickness in Nelore cattle [41].  

By identifying common chromosomal variations, we were able to pinpoint high-confidence variants, particularly highlighting the impact on immunity genes. Notably, 78 immunity genes were affected by insertions longer than 10 bp. Among these, 52 were classified as innate immunity genes, as identified through the InnateDB database, and the remaining 26 were identified as immunity-related genes based on keyword searches. Ten immunity genes were affected by longer insertions, greater than 50 bp, and eight of these were innate immunity genes, namely AP3B1, PARD3, ASCC3, RICTOR, NOX4, BANK1, RFTN1, and ANGPT1. Among these, AP3B1 is probably involved in regulating coat color in cattle and other  

domesticated mammals [27, 43, 44]. Several genes that activate the immune system, such as CD59 [45], CDH9 [46], PROCR [47], RLBP1 [48], BOLA [49], and STOM [44], respond to environmental stress and are strongly associated with responses to parasitic diseases as well [50]. Nelore macrophages control the intracellular replication of Brucella abortus more effectively, suggesting that, compared to Holstein, Nelore may possess greater natural resistance to brucellosis [51]. Additionally, we also identified 22 immunity genes affected by deletions longer than 10 bp, 16 of these being innate immunity genes. Regarding variations in the substitutions, 301 immunity genes were affected across all chromosomes, including 209 innate immunity genes and 92 immunity genes identified through keyword-based searches. Each of the immunity genes experienced more than 50 substitutions. Notably, the innate immunity genes PRKCE, EDIL3, and PRKCA were particularly affected: each had experienced over a thousand substitutions.  

It is also noteworthy that 93 immunity genes were affected by all three types of variations, namely insertions, deletions, and substitutions. Of these, 62 were innate immunity genes, of which the following 12 had experienced variations longer than 500 bp: RFTN1, ARHGAI, PLXNA4, EDIL3, AP3B1, PRKCE, PARD3, ZFPM2, ANGPT1, PTPRC, PRKCA, and TCF4. We identified variations in genes such as IFNGR1, IFNAR1, IGSF21, IL23R, and others in Nelore. Some of these gene variations have been linked to resistance to viral infections in mice and humans [52], although information on bovines remains scarce. A comparative analysis of innate and adaptive immunity in European breeds (Holstein, Brown Swiss, and Hereford) and zebu breeds (Gir, Nelore, and Guzera) was conducted by evaluating their peripheral blood leukocyte profiles, including monocytes, eosinophils, lymphocytes, CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, and CD21+ B cells. Zebu breeds, particularly Nelore, showed higher frequencies of cells involved in innate immunity, such as monocytes and non-T, non-B cells, which may explain the greater resistance shown by Nelore to some infectious and parasitic diseases [53]. Additionally, BOLA genes located on Chromosome 23, part of the bovine major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class II gene family, are promising candidates for the immune response and adaptation to tropical environments [49].  

We mapped genes affected by common chromosomal variations to taurine QTLs and identified associations with 3609 genes, including 218 immunity genes affected by insertions, 2005 genes including 131 immunity genes affected by deletions, and 7883 genes including 546 immunity genes affected by substitutions. A total of 36 QTLs were identified across chromosomes 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 16, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 24, 25, and 26 (UMD 3.1), including eight candidate genes related to feed efficiency in Nelore [54]. Additionally, 2531 CNVRs (95.5%) in Nelore overlapped by at least one base with QTLs from the Cattle Animal QTLdb. In most cases, these CNVRs were completely contained within the  

QTL regions. Among them, 482 polymorphic CNVRs overlapped with 2310 QTLs, corresponding to 282 traits, including several related to milk production. Furthermore, ten CNVRs overlapped with regions linked to meat tenderness in Nelore [55, 56]. Notably, we observed that many of the immunity genes mapped to QTLs were associated with milk, health, reproduction, meat and carcass quality, and the exterior. Specifically, we identified 67 genes affected by insertions, 42 genes affected by deletions, and 140 genes affected by substitutions that were mapped to health-related QTLs. These findings suggest that variations in immunity genes are critical not only to health but may also influence other economically important traits in cattle, such as milk production and meat quality. We also identified 46 genes within selection signature regions—genes that modulated 24 key biological processes in Nelore heifers [57]. Five of the genes – TBC1D23, NIT2, CELA1, BMPR1B, and HEXB – affect immune system pathways and inflammatory responses, and SPTBN1 is involved in the MAPK signaling pathway. These pathways are activated by inflammatory interleukins and cytokines released from damaged tissues [58, 59, 60]. Banos et al also observed correlations between traits related to immunity and those related to reproduction in dairy cattle.  

The GO enrichment analysis revealed that genes with substitution variants were highly enriched in immune-related biological processes, including positive regulation of NF-kappa B signaling and the production of key cytokines such as interleukin (IL)-6, IL-1 beta, interferon (IFNs), tumor necrosis factor (TNF) and B cell proliferation. We identified 15 immune-related genes were involved in multiple biological processes. Specifically, TLR4 and TLR9 were associated with the regulation of IL-6, IFN-gamma, TNF, and B cell proliferation. RIPK2, CARD9, and NOD2 were involved in regulating IL-6, IFN-gamma, TNF, and NF-kB signaling, while FADD contributed to the regulation of IL-6, NF-kB, and TNF. Likewise, CLEC7A and RELA were involved in the positive regulation of IL-6, IFN-gamma, and NF-kB. CD40 played a role in NF-kB activation and B cell proliferation. TNF involved in regulating IL-6, IFN-gamma, and TNF production. Members of IRAK family (IRAK1/2/3) were also involved in NF-kB-related pathways, and TRIM25 regulate the production of IFN-gamma, NF-kB, and IL-6. Furthermore, BCL2 involved in B cell signaling and proliferation, which had both deletions and substitutions. Similarly, PLCG2, observed with both deletions and substitutions, was involved in B cell signaling as well as the positive regulation of IL-6 and TNF production. Collectively, these findings suggest that structural variants, particularly substitutions, significantly impact immune-related genes involved in NF-kB signaling, cytokine production, and B cell activation. In addition, genes carrying all three types of variants (substitutions, insertions, and deletions) were enriched in the biological process of endocytosis.  

Consistent with the GO results, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis showed that genes with substitution variants were highly enriched in several immune-related pathways. These included the MAPK signaling pathway, Wnt signaling, Fc gamma R-mediated phagocytosis, T-cell receptor signaling, C-type lectin receptor signaling, chemokine signaling, Fc epsilon RI signaling, and pathways related to infections such as human papillomavirus, human T-cell leukemia virus 1, salmonella, yersinia, and bacterial invasion of epithelial cells. Other enriched pathways included inflammatory mediator regulation of TRP channels and Th17 cell differentiation. Genes with all three types of variants were also enriched in the T-cell receptor (TCR) signaling pathway. A total of 12 immune-related genes: CBLB, PIK3R1, AKT3, PAK3, NFATC2, MAPK14, NFKB1, VAV2, PTPRC, CD28, SOS1, and CARD11 were involved in this pathway. T cells are critical components of the adaptive immune system, and TCR signaling plays a central role in T cell development, homeostasis, activation, and tolerance. Proper signaling through the TCR is essential for initiating immune responses, and defects or dysregulation in this pathway have been implicated in the development of various immune-related diseases [61]. The identified genes, along with their associated biological processes and pathways, represent promising candidates for further investigation to better understand their role in the disease resistance traits of indicine cattle.  

# Conclusion  

A comprehensive comparative genomic analysis of the Bos genome is presented, emphasizing the identification of syntenic and non-syntenic regions, genomic variations between breeds, translocations in sex chromosomes, and genomic variations in immune-related genes and its associated QTLs, biological functions and pathways. Our findings highlight significant genomic variations on chromosomes X seen in comparisons between two members of each pair of breeds, and non-syntenic regions identified in four immunity genes in Nelore, namely VSTM1L, Galectin-9, HDAC5, and POLH. The chromosome-by-chromosome analysis comparing Nelore with Hereford revealed 62 immune-related genes in Nelore having insertions, deletions, and substitution, along with their association with QTLs related to milk and health. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses showed significant enrichment of immune-related biological processes and pathways, especially those involving NF-kappa B signaling, cytokine production, and T cell receptor signaling. Key immune genes such as TLR4, TLR9, RIPK2, PLCG2, FADD, CLEC7A, RELA, CD40, TRIM25, IRAK1/2/3, NOD2, BCL2, and CARD9 were identified as central players involved in multiple biological process. Altogether, these findings not only extend our understanding of the genetic basis for disease resistance in Indian cattle (Nelore breed) but also provide valuable information for developing future breeding strategies that could enhance the immunity and overall productivity of indicine cattle.
